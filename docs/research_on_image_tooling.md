# Tool design for a 4B VLM that finds brain slices in an atlas

**The literature is unanimous on one point**: small (≤10B) vision-language models cannot close fine-grained perception gaps by thinking harder in text — they close them by *manipulating pixels through tools and being rewarded for correct answers*. For the LangSlice AP-estimation problem, this means the harness should expose a compact Python-style toolbox (zoom/crop, overlay, difference, register, bisect) with a retrieval-filtered atlas interface, trained via cold-start SFT on distilled trajectories followed by GRPO with masked tool-output tokens and multi-component rewards. The strongest single prior is **DeepEyes (Zheng et al., arXiv:2505.14362)**, which lifts Qwen2.5-VL-7B by **+18.9% on V\*Bench with pure RL crop/zoom training and no SFT cold-start**, and the clearest cautionary signal is **VLM²-Bench (Zhang et al., arXiv:2502.12084)**, where even GPT-4o trails humans by **~35%** on exactly the "link matching visual cues across two images" task that AP estimation reduces to. Below I translate this evidence into a concrete design.

The rest of this review assumes familiarity with QuickNII/VisuAlign, Allen CCFv3, DeepSlice, and GRPO; I skip the expository and jump to ranked recommendations, interface principles, trajectory patterns, a red-team list, and open questions.

## 1. Recommended tool set, ranked by expected marginal value

I recommend **seven tools**, ordered by expected RLVR-measurable gain. The ranking reflects (a) how directly the tool's effect is demonstrated in the cited literature and (b) how well it amplifies rather than replaces the 4B model's reasoning capacity.

**T1. `fetch_atlas(ap_mm, atlas_name, plane, contrast)` — the retrieval primitive, extended.** Already in your baseline. Extend the return value to include a short structured caption (atlas name, AP, flagged major regions at that level, a DeepSlice-estimated AP for the atlas slice itself as a consistency anchor). The extension is motivated by two lines of work: (i) **MIRAGE / Visual Haystacks (Wu et al., arXiv:2407.13766, ICLR 2025)** — retrieval-augmented VLM search with an 8.3B backbone beats GPT-4o by **+11 percentage points** on single-needle QA over 10k-image haystacks while running ~3.4× more efficiently; (ii) **Search-R1 (Jin et al., arXiv:2503.09516)**, which shows Qwen2.5-3B gains **+20% EM** and Qwen2.5-7B **+41% EM** when trained to emit retrieval queries inside GRPO rollouts. The caption acts as grounding scaffold; the extra structured fields (region list) are what the small model actually reads — raw images alone overwhelm a 4B encoder (see §4).

**T2. `zoom(image_ref, bbox, target_size)` — the single highest-value new tool.** Every small-VLM paper that adds a zoom/crop tool reports large gains: **DeepEyes (Zheng et al., arXiv:2505.14362)** +18.9% V\*Bench on Qwen2.5-VL-7B; **"Reinforcing VLMs to Use Tools" (arXiv:2506.14821)** demonstrates the same pattern on a **3B** VLM; **ZoomEarth (arXiv:2511.12267)** beats InternVL3-8B with a 3B backbone by **+1.53%/+4.00%** on ultra-high-resolution geospatial VQA; **CropVLM (arXiv:2511.19820)** adds a 256M cropper that plugs into any VLM without fine-tuning the base. **V\* (Wu & Xie, arXiv:2312.14135)** established the pattern with SEAL (7B LLaVA backbone) outperforming GPT-4V by ~20 pts on V\*Bench. For histology, the analogue is "zoom to hippocampus/cerebellum/ventricle" — distinguishing Bregma −1.7 from −2.1 in mouse depends on dentate gyrus curvature that is invisible at 512 px global input.

**T3. `overlay(slice_img, atlas_img, alpha, transform)` — visual chain-of-thought for comparison.** **Visual Sketchpad (Hu et al., arXiv:2406.09403, NeurIPS 2024)** gives GPT-4o tools to draw masks, boxes, and overlays and reports **+9.7% on BLINK semantic correspondence**, **+12.1% on BLINK depth**, and **+14.3% on V\*Bench** — visual correspondence is *exactly* the cognitive operation AP estimation requires. The tool should accept a pre-computed affine (from a cheap phase-correlation or DeepSlice prior) so the overlay is meaningful; a raw overlay without normalization is noise. This tool has the weakest direct small-VLM evidence (Sketchpad was evaluated on GPT-4o) and is therefore a strong ablation candidate; see §5.

**T4. `difference(img_a, img_b, mode)` — with modes `{ssd, ssim, edge_xor, contour_overlay}`.** Difference maps are not formally benchmarked in the literature as first-class tools (confirmed across the surveyed work), which means there is **no paper guaranteeing they help and none showing they hurt**. They are nearly free to implement and admit verifiable intermediate signal: if the model calls `difference()` and the resulting residual energy is monotonic in |ap_query − ap_true| for nearby atlas slices, this is a natural shaping reward. I rank this above the search tools because it directly mitigates the failure mode documented in **VLM²-Bench** and **NaturalBench (Li et al., arXiv:2410.14669)** — VLMs fail at "match cue across two images," and giving them a mechanical residual image converts a matching task into a reading task.

**T5. `register(slice_img, atlas_img, method)` — call out to a real registration backbone.** The tool should wrap **DeepSlice (Carey et al., *Nat. Commun.* 14:5884, 2023)** for coronal mouse, **BrainMorph (Wang et al., arXiv:2405.14019)** or **KeyMorph (Wang et al., arXiv:2304.09941)** for cross-modality MRI-style cases, and **DINO-Reg (Song et al., arXiv:2402.15687)** as a training-free fallback (DINO-Reg placed #1 on the OncoReg challenge with frozen DINOv2 features). The tool returns (transform, keypoints, confidence). Three reasons to include this despite it being "the thing you're trying to do": (a) **DeepSlice gives human-parity coronal alignment at >1000× speed**, so it provides a strong prior the VLM can refine; (b) **SynthMorph (Hoffmann et al., arXiv:2004.10282 / 2301.11329)** demonstrates contrast-invariant registration works cross-modality, which matters for the user's 12+ atlas generalist goal; (c) **KeyMorph/BrainMorph** produce *interpretable keypoints* — letting the VLM see where the registration backbone anchored is exactly the Set-of-Mark pattern that works best (see T6). The RL risk is that the model learns to call `register()` and submit its answer verbatim, bypassing the VLM entirely; mitigation is to hold out slices where the registration backbone fails (atypical cuts, species mismatches) and penalize tool-only answers on those.

**T6. `mark_landmarks(image, labels)` — Set-of-Mark, with caveat.** Given a slice and a list of anatomical structures, overlay numbered masks (from a SAM/SEEM call or from an atlas parcellation projection). The original **Set-of-Mark (Yang et al., arXiv:2310.11441)** was devastating for GPT-4V on grounding but **Contrastive Region Guidance (Wan et al., arXiv:2403.02325, ECCV 2024)** showed it *does not transfer to open-source VLMs*, proposing instead a training-free contrastive decoding that adds **+11.1% on ViP-Bench**, **+10% on What'sUp**, **+11.5% / +7.5% on SugarCrepe**. Recommendation: implement the tool but plan the ablation — either (a) train the 4B VLM with SoM-annotated trajectories so it learns to exploit the marks (SoM-LLaVA approach), or (b) skip SoM and use CRG-style contrastive decoding against a blank-ROI baseline. For a generalist landmark VLM without SoM pretraining, **expect the raw SoM tool to underperform the landmark list returned inline with T1's structured caption.**

**T7. `bisect(ap_low, ap_high, k)` — structured search scaffolding, returns k evenly-spaced atlas slices.** Binary/k-ary search is not formally studied as a tool for VLM agents, but the cognitive load argument is strong: a 4B model cannot reliably maintain an implicit interval bound over 10+ turns. **Tree-of-Thoughts (Yao et al., arXiv:2305.10601)** showed GPT-4 goes from **4% → 74%** on Game-of-24 with explicit tree search, and **ReAct (Yao et al., arXiv:2210.03629)** established that making search state explicit in the context improves small-model success by **+34% absolute on ALFWorld**. Counter-evidence: **R1-V (Chen et al., 2025, open repo)** reports forcing `<think>` scaffolding can *hurt* a 2B Qwen2-VL by ~3.5 pts on SuperCLEVR, suggesting that for simple tasks the scaffold is parasitic. Implication: make `bisect` optional, not mandatory, and let RLVR decide when to use it. Reward both bisection and direct-estimate trajectories.

**Tools I deliberately omitted:** *chain-of-thought tool* (redundant with model's text generation; see "Mind Your Step", arXiv:2410.21333, showing CoT *degrades* multimodal face-matching by up to **36.3% absolute**), *generic image-edit tools* (rotation, contrast) not motivated by task-specific failure mode; *free-form natural-language scratchpad separate from context* (redundant with prefix tokens, adds parsing burden). Keep the surface ≤7 tools: **Gorilla's ablations (Patil et al., arXiv:2305.15334)** show small models degrade sharply once candidate tool count exceeds ~20, and small-VLM evidence converges on "fewer is better" below 7B (Section synthesis of ToolRL, Search-R1, Agent-R1).

## 2. Interface design principles

The literature is consistent across Toolformer, ToolLLM, Gorilla, ToolRL, ReTool, and Agent-R1 on several points. **Use code-style calls, not JSON-schema blobs.** Tool-R1 and ReTool both expose tools through a Python-like execution environment and report that code-native interfaces are more RL-friendly for small models than large JSON-schema zoos. **ReTool (Feng et al., arXiv:2504.11536)** reaches **67.0% AIME2024** at 400 RL steps versus 40.0% at 1080 steps for text-only RL on Qwen2.5-32B — the code interface specifically is credited for faster convergence and a **~40% shorter response length** (self-correction via executed code). For a 4B model the gain should be larger because code syntax is a highly regular pattern with ample pretraining support.

**Return compact, structured results.** MIRAGE's efficiency gain over multi-stage text pipelines comes from a compressive encoding of retrieved images. For each tool return, include (i) a fixed-field header (tool name, success/failure, timing), (ii) a payload (image handle, not raw base64 — small VLMs cannot productively re-tokenize base64), (iii) a one-line human-readable summary the model can quote. Visual Sketchpad's success on BLINK spatial (+12.1%) depends on the sketch being returned *as an image the VLM re-encodes*, not as token coordinates.

**Make errors terse and actionable.** Agent-R1 (Cheng et al., arXiv:2511.14460) formalizes tool errors as environment state; its ablations show that verbose errors with stack traces hurt small-model stability in GRPO because the policy learns to emit tool calls that *produce* informative errors (a subtle form of reward hacking). Use three error classes: `BAD_ARGS`, `TOOL_FAILED`, `RESULT_EMPTY`, each ≤20 tokens.

**Mask tool outputs from the policy-gradient loss.** This is the single most robustly replicated RLVR-tool finding. **Search-R1** introduced retrieved-token masking and shows that without it, the policy rewards itself for hallucinated retrievals. **VerlTool (arXiv:2509.01055)** and **Proof-of-Use (Ma et al., arXiv:2510.10931)** confirm this on tool-call hacking specifically. Implementation: wrap tool outputs in `<obs>...</obs>` tags and set the loss mask to zero over those token positions.

**Cap tool-call budget per episode.** Agent-R1 documents that process rewards per tool call help most when trajectory length is bounded; uncapped budgets lead to length inflation as a reward-hacking proxy. Recommend 8–12 tool calls per episode for AP estimation; bisect over 200 candidates is log₂(200)≈7.6 so 10–12 leaves headroom for zoom/overlay refinement.

**Tool naming matters more than you'd expect for 4B models.** Gorilla's retrieval-aware training and its documented hallucinated-argument reduction imply that name similarity to common API verbs (`fetch`, `zoom`, `compare`, `submit`) primes pretraining knowledge. Avoid cute project-specific names (`ap_lookup`, `slicecompare`).

## 3. Trajectory synthesis patterns for SFT warm-up

The 2024–2025 consensus for cold-start SFT on small VLMs (≤10B) is clear: **you need a cold start, and the data should come from a larger teacher or from a deterministic program, not from the student itself.**

**Pattern A — teacher distillation (recommended primary).** **Vision-R1 (Huang et al., arXiv:2503.06749)** builds a **200K** multimodal CoT dataset by "modality bridging" through DeepSeek-R1 plus a teacher MLLM, and uses it for cold-start before GRPO on a 7B VLM; Vision-R1-7B reaches 73.5% on MathVista, **0.4 pts below OpenAI o1**. Without the cold start, Vision-R1-Zero fails to converge at 7B scale. For LangSlice, the teacher can be **Gemini 2.5 Pro / GPT-5 / Claude** given images + tool definitions + ground-truth AP; sample K trajectories per training slice, filter by final-answer correctness *and* tool-call plausibility (did the zoom look at anatomically meaningful regions?), and keep the top 1–2 per example. Expected dataset: 5K–20K filtered trajectories covering the 12 atlases and 3 planes.

**Pattern B — programmatic trajectory generation.** **Toolformer (Schick et al., arXiv:2302.04761)** teaches a 6.7B GPT-J to use five APIs via self-supervised perplexity-filtered annotation and reaches near-GPT-3 zero-shot. **ToolLLM's DFSDT (Qin et al., arXiv:2307.16789)** synthesizes multi-tool trajectories by depth-first search over a tool graph; ToolLLaMA (7B) matches ChatGPT on multi-tool tasks. For AP estimation, a programmatic generator is attractive because ground-truth AP exists: start at a random AP, call bisect/zoom/overlay according to a scripted policy, label each action with its "would this narrow the interval?" answer, and produce a reference trajectory. This is the classic imitation-learning cold start and it sidesteps the cost of a commercial teacher.

**Pattern C — hybrid.** **ReTool's cold-start** uses code-replacement: start from a correct text-only reasoning chain, mechanically replace arithmetic segments with code calls. The histology analogue: start from a DeepSlice/KeyMorph-produced reasoning trace and mechanically interleave `zoom(dentate_gyrus)` and `overlay()` calls where the residual gradient is largest. This yields trajectories that are verifiably correct and tool-rich.

**Critical finding: cold-start size is smaller than you'd think.** **MM-Eureka (Meng et al., arXiv:2503.07365)** reproduces R1-Zero-style reflection on InternVL2.5-8B with only **54K RL samples** and beats a 1M-sample MPO model; MM-Eureka-Zero-38B reaches parity with 0.05% of the MPO data. Implication: don't over-invest in SFT; 5K–10K cold-start trajectories is likely sufficient before GRPO takes over. **ToolRL (Qian et al., arXiv:2504.13958)** goes further and shows **GRPO from scratch can outperform SFT-initialized RL** with the right multi-component reward (format + tool-name + param-name + param-content). Treat SFT size as an ablation axis, not a commitment.

**Reward shape for GRPO.** Four components, following ToolRL and Agent-R1:

1. **Outcome reward** — continuous function of |ap_predicted − ap_true|, e.g., Gaussian with σ = 100 µm matching your sub-mm precision target. Saturates to 1 at perfect, 0 beyond 1 mm.
2. **Confidence calibration reward** — reward for self-reported confidence matching empirical error (Brier-style). Prevents confident-wrong outputs, a noted reward-hacking failure mode.
3. **Tool-format reward** — small (+0.05) for syntactically valid tool calls; zero-sum, so it doesn't inflate.
4. **Proof-of-use reward** — following **Proof-of-Use (arXiv:2510.10931)**: reward trajectories where the final answer cites evidence from a tool call (e.g., "hippocampus matches atlas slice at 1.9 mm"). Penalize decorative tool calls with no causal link to the answer.

Do **not** reward trajectory length directly (inflation failure mode, documented across ToolRL and Proof-of-Use). Do **not** reward tool count.

## 4. Red-team list — failure modes to design against

**F1. Bag-of-words encoding of anatomy.** **Yuksekgonul et al.'s ARO benchmark (arXiv:2210.01936)** shows CLIP/BLIP perform near-random on relation and order probes despite high retrieval accuracy. For histology, this predicts that a raw SigLIP encoder will fail to distinguish "hippocampus above thalamus" from "thalamus above hippocampus" unless landmark positions are made explicit. Mitigation: T6 (landmarks in caption) and T3 (overlay).

**F2. CLIP-blind pairs.** **MMVP (Tong et al., arXiv:2401.06209)** constructed 150 high-CLIP/low-DINOv2 pairs; most open-source MLLMs score **below 25% random chance** on pair accuracy. Histology analogue: nearby AP slices (<100 µm apart) are textbook CLIP-blind pairs. Mitigation: T4 (difference), T2 (zoom), and explicit DINO features in the registration backbone (DINO-Reg, arXiv:2402.15687).

**F3. Lost-in-the-middle on multi-image input.** **MM-NIAH (Wang et al., arXiv:2406.07230)** shows Gemini-1.5 at chance on image-needle retrieval; **Visual Haystacks** shows up to **−50% accuracy drop vs single-image QA** for GPT-4o when the candidate set grows. Mitigation: never show the model more than 2–3 atlas slices simultaneously; use T7 (bisect) to serialize search, and use MIRAGE-style retrieval filtering (T1) rather than dumping 200 slices into context.

**F4. Tool-call hacking.** **Proof-of-Use (Ma et al., arXiv:2510.10931)** identifies two modes: (a) *mode collapse via tool overuse* and (b) *decorative tool calls with no causal dependence on returned evidence*. **Baker et al. (Anthropic, arXiv:2511.18397, 2025)** shows production-RL reward hacking generalizes to broader misalignment. Mitigation: evidence-linked reward (§3), mask tool-output tokens from policy loss, cap tool-call budget.

**F5. Tool-denial shortcut.** RL will discover that one-shot AP estimation from the slice alone (memorized prior) gets partial reward cheaper than tool-calling. Counter-evidence: DeepEyes succeeds *without* SFT cold-start by design because the task (V\*Bench) cannot be solved without zoom. Mitigation: mix in training slices that are demonstrably un-memorizable — novel atlases withheld from teacher-generated trajectories, species/modality combinations absent from pretraining. If the tool-denial shortcut still wins, your reward or data distribution is the problem, not the tools.

**F6. Set-of-Mark transfer failure.** **Contrastive Region Guidance (Wan et al., arXiv:2403.02325)** explicitly documents that SoM does not help open-source VLMs as it helps GPT-4V. Mitigation: do not assume SoM works out of the box; either train on SoM-annotated data or replace with CRG-style contrastive decoding against blank-ROI baseline.

**F7. CoT-induced degradation.** **"Mind Your Step" (Liu et al., arXiv:2410.21333)** reports up to **36.3% absolute accuracy drop** from CoT on visual face-matching, across all six tested models (GPT-4o, Claude 3.5 Sonnet, Claude 3 Opus, Gemini 1.5 Pro, InternVL2-26B, InternVL2-Llama3-76B). Histology face-matching analogue: comparing cytoarchitecture is closer to perceptual matching than to verbal reasoning. Mitigation: allow but do not require verbal reasoning; reward short correct trajectories equally with long correct ones.

**F8. Format fragility.** Small models fail to emit valid tool calls under distribution shift between SFT and RL. **ToolRL** specifically addresses this with its format-matching reward component (+17% over base, +15% over SFT). Mitigation: include format reward term, validate trajectory parseability pre-GRPO, and use a deterministic parser with `BAD_FORMAT` error class.

**F9. Length inflation and mode collapse in GRPO.** **MM-Doc-R1 (arXiv:2604.13579)** shows GRPO's initial-state baseline is biased in multi-turn tool rollouts and proposes similarity-weighted SPO, boosting Qwen3-4B/8B by **+6.1% / +5.0%**. Mitigation: monitor response-length histograms across training, use online difficulty filtering (MM-Eureka), consider SPO-style reweighting if standard GRPO plateaus.

**F10. Data contamination / memorization.** Train/test split must be by **atlas + slice range**, not random slice. Models can memorize specific Allen CCF slices from pretraining (the images are on the public internet). Mitigation: hold out entire AP ranges (e.g., Bregma 0.0 to −0.5) and entire atlases (e.g., all Waxholm rat slices), and report accuracy on never-seen atlases as primary metric. The **PathMMU (arXiv:2401.16355)** finding that fine-tuned open-source models can surpass GPT-4V on pathology (**>60% vs 49.8%**) *in-distribution* is a cautionary tale: OOD is where real generalization shows.

**F11. Cross-modality brittleness.** DeepSlice's stated accuracy is on coronal mouse; it may silently degrade on rat, sagittal, or non-standard stains. **SynthMorph's** success at contrast-invariance (arXiv:2004.10282) and **KeyMorph/BrainMorph's** cross-modal robustness (arXiv:2304.09941, 2405.14019) suggest the generalist goal is achievable, but only if training data spans the modalities. Mitigation: ensure every training batch mixes at least 3 atlases and 2 planes.

**F12. Confidence calibration failure.** VLMs are notoriously overconfident. Without the confidence-calibration reward (§3), `submit_estimate` will return high confidence uniformly. This matters because downstream users will gate on confidence.

## 5. Open questions for ablation

**Q1. Does `overlay` (T3) help small VLMs, or only frontier models?** Sketchpad's +14.3% on V\*Bench is GPT-4o. No paper in the surveyed literature evaluates an overlay tool on a ≤4B VLM with RLVR training. Run the ablation with and without T3; budget ~2 training runs.

**Q2. Does `bisect` (T7) help or hurt?** ToT-style scaffolding helps large models on hard tasks; R1-V shows explicit `<think>` hurts 2B VLMs on easier tasks. AP estimation is intermediate difficulty. Prediction: bisect helps on the 200-slice task but not on a 20-slice subset; worth testing.

**Q3. Raw vs DINO-Reg-conditioned registration tool (T5).** DINO-Reg is training-free and generalizes across modality; DeepSlice is modality-specific and 1000× faster with human parity on its trained distribution. Which should be the *default* method argument? No paper compares them head-to-head on histology AP estimation.

**Q4. SoM-trained vs CRG-decoded vs caption-only landmarks (T6).** Three implementations exist, none benchmarked on histology. Highest-value ablation for the landmark design question.

**Q5. Optimal SFT cold-start size.** MM-Eureka shows 54K suffices, ToolRL shows 0 can suffice with the right reward. For a 4B VLM on a novel domain, the sweet spot is unknown — sweep {0, 2K, 10K, 50K} if compute allows.

**Q6. Do pathology foundation encoders (UNI, CONCH) transfer to neuroanatomy?** UNI was trained on 100M H&E patches; CONCH (arXiv:2307.12914) has 1.17M histopath image-text pairs and gains **+17.3 pts mean text→image recall** over BiomedCLIP. Neither was trained on brain atlases specifically. Swapping SigLIP for a UNI/CONCH encoder is a strong baseline but has hidden risks (domain mismatch with non-H&E stains, atlas diagram renderings).

**Q7. Outcome-only vs process rewards for AP estimation.** ToolRL argues for fine-grained rewards; MM-Eureka argues outcome-only + KL removal + online filtering is sufficient. Verifiable AP ground truth makes outcome-only tempting, but calibration reward may still matter.

**Q8. Geo-localization recipe transfer.** **PIGEON (Haas et al., arXiv:2307.05845)** wins 6/6 against a pro GeoGuessr player using hierarchical geocells + contrastive CLIP + prototype retrieval. The AP axis is 1D but the hierarchical decomposition (atlas → plane → coarse AP → fine AP) is directly analogous. Unknown whether this beats straight regression.

**Q9. Does 4B have enough capacity for the generalist-across-12-atlases goal?** No paper directly addresses this. **Vision-R1-7B** reaches o1-parity on MathVista, so 7B clearly can; 4B is untested for this density of domain knowledge. Fallback if 4B saturates: distill from a 7B fine-tune at inference time, or stay at 4B but shrink the atlas coverage in phase 1.

## Closing synthesis

The design collapses to a familiar small-VLM-agent recipe — compact code-native toolbox, teacher-distilled cold start, GRPO with masked tool outputs and proof-of-use reward, aggressive OOD evaluation — with one domain-specific twist: your registration tool (T5) is strong enough that **the dominant risk is the model becoming a thin wrapper around DeepSlice/BrainMorph rather than a generalist reasoner.** Every design choice above is oriented to prevent that collapse while still exploiting the registration backbone where it's right. The literature says this is achievable at 4B (Search-R1 at 3B, R1-V at 2B, MM-Eureka-8B with 54K samples all demonstrate non-trivial tool-use behaviors at your scale), but the sub-millimeter precision target is aggressive and the cross-atlas generalization is beyond what any published VLM agent has attempted. That gap is the paper.
