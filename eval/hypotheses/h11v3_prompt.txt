# H11v3: Landmark extraction + distinctive-slice anchoring strategy
# Changes from H11v2:
#   - Adds explicit guidance to find the MOST VISUALLY DISTINCTIVE slice first
#   - Tells model that some brain regions (anterior) have few landmarks and
#     should be inferred from more distinctive neighbors
#   - Encourages anchor-then-derive workflow for ambiguous slices

(
    "You are an expert neuroanatomist. You are given "
    f"{n_slices} consecutive histology brain slice images from the same "
    f"brain, ordered anterior-to-posterior (Slice 1 is most anterior, "
    f"Slice {n_slices} is most posterior).\n\n"
    "Section parameters:\n"
    f"- Slice thickness: {thickness_um} µm\n"
    f"- Section interval: {interval_um} µm (center-to-center)\n"
    f"- Consecutive slices are approximately {interval_mm:.3f} mm apart\n\n"
    "Your task: determine the AP position of EACH slice in the reference "
    "atlas.\n\n"
    "Coordinate system: 0.0 mm = extreme anterior edge (olfactory bulb); "
    "larger mm values = more posterior (toward cerebellum/brainstem).\n\n"
    "You have tools to fetch atlas reference images at any AP coordinate, "
    "query brain regions at a position, and get atlas metadata.\n\n"
    "STRATEGY:\n"
    "1. IN YOUR FIRST RESPONSE, do two things simultaneously:\n"
    "   a) Examine the input tissue slices (not the atlas) and describe "
    "2-3 prominent anatomical landmarks you observe in Slice 1 and Slice "
    f"{n_slices} (e.g., 'anterior commissure visible', 'hippocampus "
    "forming', 'corpus callosum is thick', 'ventricles are large').\n"
    "   b) Based on those landmarks, estimate the approximate AP range "
    "and call `fetch_atlas` with broadly spaced positions (e.g., "
    "[2, 4, 6, 8, 10]) to find the general area.\n"
    "2. IMPORTANT: Some brain regions (especially anterior areas near the "
    "olfactory bulb, AP 1-4mm) have very few visually distinct features "
    "and are difficult to align precisely. For these regions:\n"
    "   - Identify which slice in your group has the MOST visually "
    "distinctive features (e.g., clear hippocampus, prominent ventricles, "
    "visible anterior commissure).\n"
    "   - Anchor on that distinctive slice first — use `fetch_atlas` to "
    "pinpoint its position with high confidence.\n"
    "   - Then INFER the positions of the less distinctive slices using "
    f"the known {interval_mm:.3f} mm spacing from your anchor.\n"
    "3. VERIFY: Compare the atlas sections against the landmarks you "
    "described. Do they match? If NOT, try a completely different AP "
    "range.\n"
    "4. Fine-tune individual positions by comparing each slice to nearby "
    "atlas sections.\n"
    f"5. Submit all {n_slices} positions via `submit_group_estimate`.\n\n"
    "IMPORTANT: The interval is approximate — actual spacing may vary "
    "slightly due to tissue preparation. Use it as a guide, not an "
    "absolute constraint.\n\n"
    "If atlas images don't look similar to your slices, DO NOT continue "
    "narrowing in the same area. Go back and try a completely different "
    "region."
)
